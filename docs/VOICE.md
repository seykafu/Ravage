# Ravage — Voice Bible

How each character talks, and the prose rules for every line of story
script (`src/story/beats.ts`, battle dialogue in `src/data/battles.ts`,
`src/data/campTalk.ts`). Written during the 2026-08 naturalism pass;
keep new dialogue consistent with this.

## Global prose rules

1. **Contractions in speech.** Everyone uses them except where noted
   (Corin under stress, Khione always, Archbold in public). "It is not"
   → "it isn't" unless the stiffness IS the character.
2. **One rhetorical device per speech, max.** The old script gave every
   character balanced epigrams ("Not X — Y", "X is Y, and Y is what Z").
   Kill on sight. If a scene earns one aphorism, give it to the ONE
   character whose voice it is (usually Ranatoli or Khione).
3. **No theme-statements.** Characters don't announce what the scene
   means. Cut lines that explain the story's point; trust the events.
4. **Vary length hard.** Some replies are one word. People answer
   sideways, interrupt, trail off, repeat themselves when tired.
5. **Concrete over abstract.** A soldier says "my feet are wet," not
   "the cost is real." Nouns from the character's trade (below).
6. **Parentheticals are stage business only** — small physical actions.
   Never emotional labels ("(A pause, and something in her settles.)" →
   cut or make it physical).
7. **Nobody is witty on schedule.** Jokes land badly sometimes. Leo's
   don't always work. That's the point.

## Character registers

**Amar** — plain, short, uncomfortable being looked at. Doesn't
philosophize; when he tries, it comes out awkward and he stops. Anger =
quieter, more specific. Farm vocabulary early (chores, weather, animals),
military creeping in later. Never gives speeches; gives instructions.
Says "I don't know" out loud.

**Lucian** — old soldier, teaches by chore. Gruff, affection through
instruction and complaint. Sentences like drill counts. Calls Amar "boy"
when it matters.

**Ning** — youngest, village archer. Earnest, blurts, counts things
(arrows, sacks of grain, days since home). Plain words, no metaphors —
when she reaches for one it's from farming and slightly wrong. Asks the
question everyone's avoiding.

**Maya** — fixer. Clipped, transactional, allergic to sentiment. Deflects
with terms-of-trade language ("what's it cost", "who's paying"). When
she's sincere it's short and flat and she changes the subject fast.
Sarcasm dry, not quippy.

**Leo** — duelist, needles people, jokes at the wrong moment, brags to
cover fear and everyone knows it. Sincerity comes out sideways or in a
rush, immediately regretted. Talks a bit too much.

**Ranatoli** — old bear. Proverbs, war stories with suspicious numbers,
teases the young ones, big appetites. His motto ("we bleed together or
we feast together") is HIS — nobody else gets lines that shaped. Calls
people lad, girl, Captain-my-Captain.

**Selene** — huntress. Minimum words. Reports what she saw: tracks,
wind, smoke, birds. No abstractions, no similes. When she says something
personal it's three words and she leaves.

**Veya** — lens scholar. Precise, technical, impatient with vagueness.
Thinks in measurements and corrects people's imprecision mid-sentence.
Humor bone-dry, usually about optics. Rambles when excited, catches
herself.

**Corin** — ex-imperial knight. Formal register, rank habits, procedure
as grief-management. Clipped military syntax ("Two riders. South gate.").
Contractions only when he's cracking. Never uses ten words where four
are regulation.

**Ndara** — commander. Command voice: assessment, order, done. Approval
is one word. Doesn't explain herself twice. Dry about her own coma.

**Khione** — ancient. Allowed strangeness: old rhythms, no contractions,
images from seas and time. But concrete images, not fortune cookies —
she remembers SPECIFIC things (a shore, a winter, a name). She should
unsettle, not soothe.

**Madame Dawn** — politician. Warm, practiced, speaks in speeches ON
PURPOSE (in-world: she rehearses). In private the warmth drops and the
sentences get short. The contrast is the characterization.

**Archbold** — king. Public register formal, weary. Private register
(late arcs) plainer than anyone expects.

**Narrator (N beats)** — close-third, past-tense-feeling present. Keep
the ledger/price imagery HERE if anywhere — it's the book's narrator
voice, not the characters'. Even so: fewer semicolon aphorisms, more
picture.

## Banned constructions (from the old script)

- "Not X — Y." / "It is not X. It is Y." (everyone had it; now nobody
  does, except the narrator sparingly)
- "X is what Y looks like, given enough time"-shaped definitions
- Characters quoting the game's themes ("the ledger", "the price")
  unless it's Maya being literal about money or the narrator
- "(He looks at X.)" as emotional shorthand more than once a scene
- Everyone ending speeches on the strong word. Let lines end weak
  sometimes; people do.
